# BFladderbean / Phase study

This is the single visual system for the Next.js site. It replaces the former IRIS/GLACIER/ABYSS themes. `AGENTS.md` defines product scope; this file describes the implemented visual language.

## Direction

Swiss alignment and a restrained grid meet the high-contrast, sliced visual tension of Arknights chapter 17, “Critical Phase Transition.” Arcadia informs native snap scenes, visibility-driven scene state and a direct pointer, not the site's identity or content. No game artwork or logos are copied.

The opening is a full paper canvas, not a centered card: an oversized Syne handle, five compact lines of confirmed personal information indented beneath it, and a long rule ending in a visible scroll label and double chevron. Opaque acid and violet planes meet at the canvas edges; no floating circles, rotating objects, ornamental microcopy or continuous arrow bounce. Opening and navigation lead into one scene per recent article (up to three, newest first), followed by a Seurat pointillist closing landscape. Its image-bound, pointer-reactive dots are deliberate artwork, not the former ambient particle effect. No terminal dashboard, crosshair, scanning lines, random transition, decorative CSS gradients, theme switch, or generic card grid. The writing title's interactive color wipe is the sole gradient treatment.

The homepage has no global header or separate wordmark; the opening's @BFladderbean is its primary identity, and the Navigation scene owns its five primary destinations. Every non-home route, including articles and 404s, has a shared top navigation in normal document flow: the site name links home, the five destinations reuse the local navigation data, and the current page or article's All Posts section is underlined and announced with `aria-current`. The header does not overlay reading content. The homepage side scene picker is the sole visible scene-progress indicator, with no numeric counter. Scene names remain announced to assistive technology.

## Tokens

| Semantic role | Tailwind theme token | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Reading / opening canvas | `--color-paper` | `#f5f5ef` |
| Text / closing canvas | `--color-ink` | `#17171a` |
| Edge planes / closing type | `--color-acid` | `#d9f542` |
| Navigation canvas / personal-information emphasis | `--color-violet` | `#4930bc` |
| Deep violet | `--color-violet-dark` | `#27165e` |
| Secondary reading text | `--color-muted` | `#62625e` |
| Editorial divider | `--color-line` | `#cecec6` |

These are scene roles in one theme, not selectable palettes. Color never carries scene state alone: the elongated scene tick and `aria-current` provide independent indicators.

Motion tokens stay equally small: `--ease-smooth` (`cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1)`) for content reveals and `--ease-panel` (`cubic-bezier(0.76, 0, 0.24, 1)`) for the intro panel sweep. Utilities reference them as `ease-smooth` and `ease-panel`; animation code that reads computed custom properties still uses the root aliases `--ease` and `--ease-panel`. Durations are named at their site of use — 620ms for that sweep and the violet handoff into the first article, 680ms for scene content settling, 520ms for the title color wipe, section-label darkening and cover zoom, 180ms for cover pointer settling, 480ms or shorter for the navigation blocks sequenced against the sweep.

## Type and grid

- Typography: locally hosted Syne throughout display, navigation, UI and article prose, with real 400/500/600/700/800 weights loaded by `next/font/local`. Body, scene and reading tokens share this family; there is no separate Arial or serif reading face.
- Opening: the handle, biography and scroll cue all use Syne; the scroll cue uses the loaded 700 weight.
- Reading: Syne at 18px and 1.95 line-height on desktop. KaTeX retains its own math fonts; code retains system monospace.
- Side gutter: `clamp(24px, 5vw, 88px)`; document shell at most 1440px. Article body at most 760px.
- Spacing scale: 8/16/24/40/64px, with fluid display sizes and scene-dependent gaps.
- Writing-scene type follows each cell's content-box width and height, not the whole viewport. Title and ordinal sizes are capped at 144px and 160px; section labels, descriptions and metadata have their own bounded scales (24–44px, 22–36px and 18–26px), with All posts at 22–32px instead of the global 14px text-link size. These are hierarchy bounds, not targets to fill every rectangle. The three grid variants share the scales; the shallow title band also respects its available height.
- Layout: the opening uses a centered composition at most 1000px wide, with left-aligned display type and an indented biography; edge planes occupy separate space rather than sitting behind the text. Closing abandons the standalone Mondrian block: a full-scene pointillist interpretation of Seurat's public-domain La Grande Jatte sits behind a single left-aligned column of heading and social links. Sampled dots are dimmer on the left for readability, while the right preserves figures, trees and riverbank. Navigation splits left and right: a plain `Navigation` title in the left column and five full-width rows in the right column, both vertically centered in the violet field. It has no slogan; each row keeps its right-edge arrow and the labels keep the pointer-local dispersion. Each recent article still owns a full-scene Mondrian field: flat paper, acid, violet and ink rectangles separated by heavy ink rules, sized in viewport fractions with no inner max-width. A violet block names the item, a paper block carries its ordinal, an acid block holds the original-language title and reading arrow, paper holds the description, ink carries date and All posts, and the photograph fills its assigned plate. The three scenes re-cut that field rather than repeat a template. Other routes use a quiet paper canvas and generous title spacing.

## About: a private corner

`src/app/about/page.tsx` is a server-rendered, casual English personal page, not a research profile or equal-column biography. The existing `illustration.png` is the owner's OC and shares the opening with the greeting. Its caption does not invent a character name, backstory, illustrator credit, or claim the owner drew it. Machine-learning text comes from the owner's account: experimental uncertainty, skepticism expressed as a personal position, and the limits on a young researcher's choices. The longer reflection uses a native keyboard-operable disclosure so it need not dominate the first reading; its content height eases open and closed on the site curve, with reduced motion keeping the native instant toggle.

Graphic design, games, home networking, Minecraft source code, blogging and chatting with online friends form differently sized, offset fragments, not skills or a forced explanation of the research. Syne and the existing paper/ink/violet/acid tokens stay shared; no alternate theme is introduced. Research follows a descending reading order: a full-width heading, a left-aligned equation, then the lower right prose. Most content now carries a restrained, static tilt: opening title, OC caption, research heading, blogging and goodbye −2°; welcome +1.5°; research prose +1°; equation and design −3°; off-the-clock heading +2°; games and chatting +3°; home networking −1.5°; Minecraft −2°. The greeting remains −3° and the OC frame +2°. Each fragment rotates as a unit, not as independently tilted text and icons. The game title uses the same ink token as the other hobby headings, with violet reserved for the linework and existing accents. Staggered game/Minecraft spacing and an inset router fragment follow the thread's lower sweep. The OC frame and acid `Aa` specimen remain complete rectangles without arbitrary clipped corners.

One continuous violet curve sweeps through the composition and can cross text, without opaque text masks. `AboutEntry` anchors its turns to measured content bounds, including the router/Minecraft gap and blogging fragment on the return to goodbye. A chord-parameterized natural cubic spline solves both coordinate second derivatives across the entire thread, replacing local Lagrange blending. Position, tangent and second derivative are continuous at joins; the spline minimizes squared parameter-space second derivatives, not geometric curvature, and does not itself guarantee convexity. Broad outward turns come from the anchor layout: the OC-to-research passage descends through a long open arc, the game return follows a tilted open ellipse rather than a closed circle, and the goodbye turn starts earlier and farther left with an off-canvas exit. Only necessary direction changes remain; outward-convex spans are preferred over repeated inward dents. The spline is sampled at three-unit parameter intervals into SVG `M`/`L` coordinates, without manually authored Bézier controls. `ResizeObserver` updates anchors for disclosure expansion and font/window changes. Direct loads, reloads, reduced motion and forced colors skip the entrance and show the complete page immediately.

The composition is centered and capped at 1440px. Columns use zero-minimum grid tracks, the opening title scales against its own column rather than an ultrawide viewport, and layouts at 760px and below return to one-column reading. Nothing relies on a fixed document height. Supplementary hobby artwork uses Lucide's Gamepad 2, Router and Pickaxe SVGs, all in violet with matching 1.25-unit strokes and no individual rotation. Sources and the ISC notice are in `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`.

The opening's lower-left space contains an original static wireframe of the hyperbolic paraboloid `z = x² − y²`, not a generic orbit symbol or repeated BF wordmark. Two families of exact quadratic sections form the projected mesh; the two central sections have heavier violet strokes, with a small equation beneath. It introduces a mathematical motif without implying a research result or adding continuous motion. The OC sits in a white, acid-taped frame over a slightly offset violet backing. Research prose starts closer to its equation, accompanied by a quiet Guess / Experiment / Try again strip. The hobby fragments gain content-related print details: construction lines and palette swatches for typography, an acid controller backing, a dotted router field, a stepped pickaxe edge and a ruled blogging slip. The chatting fragment has no quotation mark or independent vertical rule; only the continuous page thread crosses it, keeping one line language. These details remain static and use the existing tokens. Decorative opening microcopy such as “Still figuring things out” is omitted: words must communicate information, not fill space. The off-the-clock jump is a compact, content-sized text-and-arrow link, with underlining limited to the text on hover and keyboard focus. The arrow does not inherit an underline or create a tall empty clickable block. The fragment link uses Next Link so browser history retains the correct route state; its downward scroll eases on the site curve, yields to wheel, touch or scroll-key input, and falls back to an instant jump under reduced motion.

## Interaction responsibilities

- `HomeDeck.tsx`: native scroll container and CSS snap; unmodified intersection ratios choose `past / active / future`, following Arcadia's scene controller. All scene sections remain in normal flow; only the independent decorative background persists. Every selection — the opening cue, the side picker, keyboard arrows, Page Up/Down, Home/End, space and a discrete wheel notch — is a native smooth scroll to that scene's own snap target, so the browser retargets an in-flight scroll from its current position instead of queueing one. Pixel trackpad gestures stay native and are never intercepted, and a gesture that stops short of the next scene simply returns to where it started. Keyboard movement is suppressed inside links and controls; at high zoom tall scenes keep ordinary keyboard scrolling. Focus does not overwrite the navigation target. Actual dimension changes preserve the last observed scene; initial resize observations do not reposition the deck. A restored scene (session storage or URL fragment) is published as active before the enhancement flag, so a reload shows that scene's own state instead of replaying a reveal. Each scene owns its normal snap target; there are no sticky scene sections, synthetic visibility corrections or extra snap anchors.
- Scene CSS: the opening and navigation share one pinned canvas. The paper canvas and its edge planes stay fixed while the two intro scenes travel over them, and the violet rectangle grows from an edge rect into the navigation canvas. Scene state owns the choreography, not scroll offset: the panel sweeps to full bleed over 620ms with `cubic-bezier(0.76, 0, 0.24, 1)`, and the opening composition leaves a beat earlier and returns a beat later so it is never read against a half-covered canvas. A gesture that stops short of the navigation scene changes no state, so a half-played sequence is never rewound, and no part of the sequence waits for scroll distance that has nothing left to show. The navigation reveal is sequenced against the sweep rather than merely delayed: the link column waits for the region the panel reaches at roughly 60% of the expansion, so it is never drawn against the paper canvas the panel is covering. Each exit takes its wait from the state it is leaving, so the panel uncovers the links on the retreat. The navigation's violet is one field rather than two screens: the same rectangle travels in with the first article, covering it edge to edge, and tightens into that article's top-left label cell on the same 620ms panel curve, so the grid is unveiled from its outer cells inward instead of swapped behind a flash. The contraction is a compositor transform from a scene-sized start (anchored at the scene's top-left, with a few percent of overscan clipped by the scene), not a layout animation from off-canvas, so the visible shrink spans the whole curve instead of starting halfway through it. That article's grid does not travel on entry, so no paper seam opens between the two violets while the deck is moving. The field is a grid item of the article's own Mondrian field, so its resting rectangle is the label cell's rectangle exactly and no second measurement of the ink rules exists; the label cell paints above it, so the section name reads the whole way in. As a decorative overlay it takes no pointer events, and the label cell sits above the whole-scene link, which is why it passes its own clicks through to it. Later scenes keep their 680ms content settling curve. Reduced motion, forced colors or a browser without JavaScript keep ordinary readable scenes: the navigation scene carries its own violet background, no plane or content state is applied, and the introduction is visible immediately, with its scroll cue as a semantic fragment link. The violet handoff is absent under the same conditions, and in the narrow stacked layout, where the article's cells are no longer a field to tighten into.
- Closing handoff: the latest-work sequence does not travel into the closing. The closing scene is held on the same canvas as the articles, and its ink grows out of the last article's ink (date / All posts) plate: a `clip-path` from that plate's rectangle to full bleed on the 620ms panel curve, mirroring the navigation's violet tightening into the lead article. `useClosingCut` measures the plate with layout offsets (unaffected by hold and morph transforms) and publishes it as `--closing-cut`. The landscape surfaces at 360ms, the heading at 460ms and the links at 540ms; leaving shrinks the ink back into the plate and keeps the closing drawn until it lands, so reversing mid-sweep retargets the same transition. Reduced motion, forced colors, narrow layouts and browsers without scroll-driven animations keep the ordinary travelling closing.
- `Navigation.tsx`: semantic Next links, native View Transition API where available, immediate router navigation; no custom history/router or loading-mask substitute. A browser without the API navigates normally. History is owned by Next/browser. Homepage restores its last observed scene through optional session storage.
- Writing handoff: opening any recent article from its title plate is a two-beat route transition owned by the navigation layer, not a root cross-fade. `measureArticleCurtain` reads the active writing scene's plate, title and centered destination rectangles before the route changes; a fixed violet plate scales from the slab to full bleed on the 620ms panel curve while a matching paper title travels to the viewport center. Navigation starts only after the 620ms cover has landed, so no partially covered edge can reveal the incoming page; the route then commits under the still-covered field. Once the cover is full and the article pathname is present, the whole violet field translates left over 520ms on a fast-out curve and uncovers the article, with a short 11px blur pulse at the start of the travel that falls back to sharp before the plate clears the canvas. `transitionend` drives both beats with timer fallbacks, so a missed event or slow route never strands a covered canvas. Reduced motion and forced colors skip the overlay and use ordinary navigation. The overlay draws the handoff itself, so the root View Transition is not started for this route.
- First-level roll: following a home Explore link to About, Contact, Projects, Friend Links or All Posts plays a deploy.re-style band curtain owned by `NavigationProvider` (`.route-roll`). Eight full-width horizontal bands unroll from the left edge with `scaleX`, top band first, 340ms each, 28ms apart; each band paints a horizontal `--violet` → `--paper` gradient, so the unrolling front is a bright paper edge and the closed cover reads as one left-violet, right-paper sheet. The route commits only once every band has landed; the bands then roll off to the right, bottom band first, 20ms apart. While covered, `html[data-route-curtain]` holds `main.page-shell` at opacity 0; after the last band clears, `data-route-entry` plays the shared 480ms `page-entry` rise, and each page's own entrance (e.g. the About thread draw) starts. `transitionend` on the last band drives each beat, with timer fallbacks. Reduced motion and forced colors skip the curtain and the entry and navigate immediately.
- `AboutEntry.tsx`: `Navigation.tsx` marks a client-side or history entry to `/about` before the route commits. The content-anchored thread uses `pathLength="1"` and a 1400ms Web Animations draw at even screen-space speed to just beyond the viewport; marked blocks then reveal once through an intersection observer. Direct loads have no entry state, and reduced motion or forced colors clears it immediately. The controller never changes scroll position, locks input or delays navigation, and the root View Transition is skipped for this route.
- `CustomCursor.tsx`: position set directly from mouse coordinates, no spring/trailing interpolation. Interactive hover becomes an outlined circle; press becomes a compressed slab; prose gets an I-beam-like shape. The pointer cannot receive events. Native cursor remains until valid mouse coordinates initialize the custom layer, and returns on leave, blur, unsupported input or forced colors. Re-entry/focus restores the custom pointer when its known position remains valid; route changes refresh targeting and clear pressed state. A nonzero capture box preserves the pointer's named view-transition surface.
- `ExploreLinks.tsx`: semantic navigation split into two columns. The left column carries a plain `Navigation` title; the right column carries five full-width rows separated by rules, each with a right-edge arrow. Hover and keyboard focus turn the row acid. Labels retain a paper-colored core with opposing acid/deep-violet fringes: each glyph's split follows its distance and direction from the pointer with an elliptical 180px/72px falloff, no uniform row-wide split and no autonomous flicker. Coordinates are read before style writes and updates are coalesced into one animation frame; pointer leave/cancel, blur and accessibility-mode changes reset the effect. Reduced motion, coarse pointers and forced colors disable dispersion. Links keep their normal accessible names and focus outline.
- Writing hover: the title plate is the sole hover trigger. A solid violet overlay wipes across the acid plate from the left over 520ms; no intermediate hue or soft gradient band is painted, so every frame stays on the two palette colors. Title and arrow use `background-clip: text` / a CSS mask with a hard paper/ink stop driven by the same registered `--sweep` value in slab coordinates (`--slab-w`, per-element `--ox`), so a glyph changes color exactly under the wipe edge instead of splitting ahead of the background. Settled states: ink on acid 14.6:1, paper on violet 7.9:1. In the same 520ms the section label's plate settles from violet to a violet/deep-violet mix (40% violet in OKLab); its type stays paper. Other plates keep their full color and opacity: dimming them over the ink rules turned paper into mid grey and cut ink text from 16.3:1 to 6.5:1. The label keeps its light type and never flips to acid. Keyboard focus gets the same sweep plus a visible paper inset outline. The title is the sole accessible article link, with an unfocusable stretched link keeping the remaining scene clickable; All posts retains its own destination. `useWritingCover.ts` listens on the active article, because the stretched link sits above the decorative image. Inside the cover only, mouse position pans the image by at most 14px per axis with an 8% zoom; the frame and Mondrian rules never move. Pointer leave/cancel, scrolling, scene changes, blur, resize and accessibility-mode changes reset it. Updates are coalesced per animation frame, no idle animation loop. Reduced motion applies the hover state immediately without sweep travel and disables image motion; coarse pointers disable image motion; forced colors drop the wipe paint (system text and a `LinkText` arrow) and image motion.
- `PointillistLandscape.tsx`: a local, cropped WebP of Seurat's painting supplies colors to a seeded jittered dot lattice (approximately 43,000 dots on a large desktop canvas). The static drawing is cached; only the disturbed region is redrawn over it. Mouse proximity within 125 CSS pixels gently pushes and twists dots, then they return to their anchors. No autonomous drift: animation stops once settled, offscreen, or hidden. Canvas never receives pointer input; listeners belong to the scene. Reduced motion and coarse pointers keep the static composition, forced colors hides it, and a prerendered pointillist WebP remains visible without JavaScript or canvas initialization.
- Reduced motion: native immediate scene selection, no layer travel/reveal offsets, no spatial route transitions. Content remains readable and all navigation available.
- Keyboard focus is visible; a skip link reaches the main content. External links announce opening a new tab. Empty project/friend/contact fields are explicit and do not invent people or accounts.

## Reading and content

Article pages never inherit snap scrolling, opening edge planes, changing scene colors or reveal motion. Articles, including titles and descriptions, are written in English. Headings are deep-linkable; formulas, code, tables, quotes and lists are server-rendered from Markdown. Tags and categories remain in source metadata but are not navigation features.

Homepage covers use the optional `heroImage` frontmatter string: a filename selects an existing image in `src/assets/`, while a root-relative URL selects a file under `public/`. Next Image reserves the cover area and serves appropriately sized images. Covers are decorative beside the article title; articles without a cover show their real description in the same composition, not an invented image. The current three articles reference existing NASA photographs. This cover treatment does not change article-page headers; a shared article header remains a separate design task.

## Verification scope

Desktop browser verification is required for changes; the final delivery lists actual observations and commands. CSS includes narrow-window fallbacks to avoid deliberate blocking, but mobile support and real touch hardware are not accepted delivery claims. Physical mouse-wheel and touchpad feel still require real-device judgment; synthetic browser input is not hardware performance proof.
