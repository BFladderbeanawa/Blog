# BFladderbean / Phase study

This is the single visual system for the Next.js site. It replaces the former IRIS/GLACIER/ABYSS themes. `AGENTS.md` defines product scope; this file describes the implemented visual language.

## Direction

Swiss alignment and a restrained grid meet the high-contrast, sliced visual tension of Arknights chapter 17, “Critical Phase Transition.” Arcadia informs native snap scenes, visibility-driven scene state and a direct pointer, not the site's identity or content. No game artwork or logos are copied.

The opening is a full paper canvas, not a centered card: an oversized Syne handle, five compact lines of confirmed personal information indented beneath it, and a long rule ending in a visible scroll label and double chevron. Opaque acid and violet planes meet at the canvas edges; no floating circles, rotating objects, ornamental microcopy or continuous arrow bounce. Opening and navigation lead into one scene per recent article (up to three, newest first), followed by a Seurat pointillist closing landscape. Its image-bound, pointer-reactive dots are deliberate artwork, not the former ambient particle effect. No terminal dashboard, crosshair, scanning lines, random transition, CSS gradients, theme switch, or generic card grid.

The homepage has no global header or separate wordmark; the opening's @BFladderbean is its primary identity, and the Navigation scene owns its five primary destinations. Every non-home route, including articles and 404s, has a shared top navigation in normal document flow: the site name links home, the five destinations reuse the local navigation data, and the current page or article's All Posts section is underlined and announced with `aria-current`. The header does not overlay reading content. The homepage side scene picker is the sole visible scene-progress indicator, with no numeric counter. Scene names remain announced to assistive technology.

## Tokens

| Semantic role | CSS token | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Reading / opening canvas | `--paper` | `#f5f5ef` |
| Text / closing canvas | `--ink` | `#17171a` |
| Edge planes / closing type | `--acid` | `#d9f542` |
| Navigation canvas / personal-information emphasis | `--violet` | `#4930bc` |
| Deep violet | `--violet-dark` | `#27165e` |
| Secondary reading text | `--muted` | `#62625e` |
| Editorial divider | `--line` | `#cecec6` |

These are scene roles in one theme, not selectable palettes. Color never carries scene state alone: the elongated scene tick and `aria-current` provide independent indicators.

Motion tokens stay equally small: `--ease` (`cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1)`) for content reveals and `--ease-panel` (`cubic-bezier(0.76, 0, 0.24, 1)`) for the intro panel sweep. Durations are named at their site of use — 620ms for that sweep and the violet handoff into the first article, 680ms for scene content settling, 720ms for the cover's hover scale, 480ms or shorter for the navigation blocks sequenced against the sweep.

## Type and grid

- Display: existing locally hosted Syne, weights 400/700/800. Heavy, tightly spaced, large Latin display; CSS uses Next's local font loader. Homepage article scenes set all of their own type in Syne, falling back to Microsoft YaHei / PingFang SC for Chinese, so a scene reads as one geometric-sans voice; article pages keep Noto Serif SC for reading.
- Opening: the handle, biography and scroll cue all use Syne; the scroll cue uses the loaded 700 weight rather than a separate UI face or synthetic 600 weight.
- UI: Arial with Microsoft YaHei / PingFang SC fallbacks outside the article scenes. No decorative monospace labels.
- Reading: locally bundled Noto Serif SC, 400/600, 18px and 1.95 line-height on desktop. KaTeX uses its own math fonts. Code uses system monospace.
- Side gutter: `clamp(24px, 5vw, 88px)`; document shell at most 1440px. Article body at most 760px.
- Spacing scale: 8/16/24/40/64px, with fluid display sizes and scene-dependent gaps.
- Writing-scene type follows each cell's content-box width and height, not the whole viewport. Title and ordinal sizes are capped at 144px and 160px; section labels, descriptions and metadata have their own bounded scales (24–44px, 22–36px and 18–26px), with All posts at 22–32px instead of the global 14px text-link size. These are hierarchy bounds, not targets to fill every rectangle. The three grid variants share the scales; the shallow title band also respects its available height. Syne and the CJK sans fallback remain unchanged.
- Layout: the opening uses a centered composition at most 1000px wide, with left-aligned display type and an indented biography; edge planes occupy separate space rather than sitting behind the text. Closing abandons the standalone Mondrian block: a full-scene pointillist interpretation of Seurat's public-domain La Grande Jatte sits behind a single left-aligned column of heading and social links. Sampled dots are dimmer on the left for readability, while the right preserves figures, trees and riverbank. Navigation splits heading and five links, with its heading vertically centered against the full list. Each recent article still owns a full-scene Mondrian field: flat paper, acid, violet and ink rectangles separated by heavy ink rules, sized in viewport fractions with no inner max-width. A violet block names the item, a paper block carries its ordinal, an acid block holds the original-language title and reading arrow, paper holds the description, ink carries date and All posts, and the photograph fills its assigned plate. The three scenes re-cut that field rather than repeat a template. Other routes use a quiet paper canvas and generous title spacing.

## Interaction responsibilities

- `HomeDeck.tsx`: native scroll container and CSS snap; unmodified intersection ratios choose `past / active / future`, following Arcadia's scene controller. All scene sections remain in normal flow; only the independent decorative background persists. Every selection — the opening cue, the side picker, keyboard arrows, Page Up/Down, Home/End, space and a discrete wheel notch — is a native smooth scroll to that scene's own snap target, so the browser retargets an in-flight scroll from its current position instead of queueing one. Pixel trackpad gestures stay native and are never intercepted, and a gesture that stops short of the next scene simply returns to where it started. Keyboard movement is suppressed inside links and controls; at high zoom tall scenes keep ordinary keyboard scrolling. Focus does not overwrite the navigation target. Actual dimension changes preserve the last observed scene; initial resize observations do not reposition the deck. A restored scene (session storage or URL fragment) is published as active before the enhancement flag, so a reload shows that scene's own state instead of replaying a reveal. Each scene owns its normal snap target; there are no sticky scene sections, synthetic visibility corrections or extra snap anchors.
- Scene CSS: the opening and navigation share one pinned canvas. The paper canvas and its edge planes stay fixed while the two intro scenes travel over them, and the violet rectangle grows from an edge rect into the navigation canvas. Scene state owns the choreography, not scroll offset: the panel sweeps to full bleed over 620ms with `cubic-bezier(0.76, 0, 0.24, 1)`, and the opening composition leaves a beat earlier and returns a beat later so it is never read against a half-covered canvas. A gesture that stops short of the navigation scene changes no state, so a half-played sequence is never rewound, and no part of the sequence waits for scroll distance that has nothing left to show. The navigation reveal is sequenced against the sweep rather than merely delayed: the link column waits for the region the panel reaches at roughly 60% of the expansion and the heading for the region it reaches at roughly 80%, so neither block is ever drawn against the paper canvas the panel is covering. Each exit takes its wait from the state it is leaving, so the panel uncovers the two blocks in the same order it covered them. The navigation's violet is one field rather than two screens: the same rectangle travels in with the first article, covering it edge to edge, and tightens into that article's top-left label cell on the same 620ms panel curve, so the grid is unveiled from its outer cells inward instead of swapped behind a flash. The contraction is a compositor transform from a scene-sized start (anchored at the scene's top-left, with a few percent of overscan clipped by the scene), not a layout animation from off-canvas, so the visible shrink spans the whole curve instead of starting halfway through it. That article's grid does not travel on entry, so no paper seam opens between the two violets while the deck is moving. The field is a grid item of the article's own Mondrian field, so its resting rectangle is the label cell's rectangle exactly and no second measurement of the ink rules exists; the label cell paints above it, so the section name reads the whole way in. As a decorative overlay it takes no pointer events, and the label cell sits above the whole-scene link, which is why it passes its own clicks through to it. Later scenes keep their 680ms content settling curve. Reduced motion, forced colors or a browser without JavaScript keep ordinary readable scenes: the navigation scene carries its own violet background, no plane or content state is applied, and the introduction is visible immediately, with its scroll cue as a semantic fragment link. The violet handoff is absent under the same conditions, and in the narrow stacked layout, where the article's cells are no longer a field to tighten into.
- Closing handoff: the latest-work sequence does not travel into the closing. The closing scene is held on the same canvas as the articles, and its ink grows out of the last article's ink (date / All posts) plate: a `clip-path` from that plate's rectangle to full bleed on the 620ms panel curve, mirroring the navigation's violet tightening into the lead article. `useClosingCut` measures the plate with layout offsets (unaffected by hold and morph transforms) and publishes it as `--closing-cut`. The landscape surfaces at 360ms, the heading at 460ms and the links at 540ms; leaving shrinks the ink back into the plate and keeps the closing drawn until it lands, so reversing mid-sweep retargets the same transition. Reduced motion, forced colors, narrow layouts and browsers without scroll-driven animations keep the ordinary travelling closing.
- `Navigation.tsx`: semantic Next links, native View Transition API where available, immediate router navigation; no custom history/router or loading-mask substitute. A browser without the API navigates normally. History is owned by Next/browser. Homepage restores its last observed scene through optional session storage.
- `CustomCursor.tsx`: position set directly from mouse coordinates, no spring/trailing interpolation. Interactive hover becomes an outlined circle; press becomes a compressed slab; prose gets an I-beam-like shape. The pointer cannot receive events. Native cursor remains until valid mouse coordinates initialize the custom layer, and returns on leave, blur, unsupported input or forced colors. Re-entry/focus restores the custom pointer when its known position remains valid; route changes refresh targeting and clear pressed state. A nonzero capture box preserves the pointer's named view-transition surface.
- `ExploreLinks.tsx`: navigation labels retain a paper-colored core with opposing acid/deep-violet fringes. Each glyph's horizontal and vertical split follows its own distance and direction from the pointer, with an elliptical 180px/72px falloff, no uniform row-wide split and no autonomous flicker. Coordinates are read before style writes and updates are coalesced into one animation frame; pointer leave/cancel, blur and accessibility-mode changes reset the effect. Reduced motion, coarse pointers and forced colors disable dispersion. Links keep their normal accessible names, focus outline and semantic navigation.
- `PointillistLandscape.tsx`: a local, cropped WebP of Seurat's painting supplies colors to a seeded jittered dot lattice (approximately 43,000 dots on a large desktop canvas). The static drawing is cached; only the disturbed region is redrawn over it. Mouse proximity within 125 CSS pixels gently pushes and twists dots, then they return to their anchors. No autonomous drift: animation stops once settled, offscreen, or hidden. Canvas never receives pointer input; listeners belong to the scene. Reduced motion and coarse pointers keep the static composition, forced colors hides it, and a prerendered pointillist WebP remains visible without JavaScript or canvas initialization.
- Reduced motion: native immediate scene selection, no layer travel/reveal offsets, no spatial route transitions. Content remains readable and all navigation available.
- Keyboard focus is visible; a skip link reaches the main content. External links announce opening a new tab. Empty project/friend/contact fields are explicit and do not invent people or accounts.

## Reading and content

Article pages never inherit snap scrolling, opening edge planes, changing scene colors or reveal motion. Chinese articles stay Chinese. Headings are deep-linkable; formulas, code, tables, quotes and lists are server-rendered from Markdown. Tags and categories remain in source metadata but are not navigation features.

Homepage covers use the optional `heroImage` frontmatter string: a filename selects an existing image in `src/assets/`, while a root-relative URL selects a file under `public/`. Next Image reserves the cover area and serves appropriately sized images. Covers are decorative beside the article title; articles without a cover show their real description in the same composition, not an invented image. The current three articles reference existing NASA photographs. This cover treatment does not change article-page headers; a shared article header remains a separate design task.

## Verification scope

Desktop browser verification is required for changes; the final delivery lists actual observations and commands. CSS includes narrow-window fallbacks to avoid deliberate blocking, but mobile support and real touch hardware are not accepted delivery claims. Physical mouse-wheel and touchpad feel still require real-device judgment; synthetic browser input is not hardware performance proof.
